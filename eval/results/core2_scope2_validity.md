### High Severity Findings

*   **Only one Scope 2 figure (location-based); no market-based total**
    *   *What's wrong:* All 18 rows use grid-average emission factors. Although renewable percentages (0–45%) are recorded, they are never applied; proper accounting using valid contractual instruments could change the market-based electricity total by an estimated 75.1 tCO2e.
    *   *Why it matters:* The GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance requires dual reporting of both location-based and market-based figures to reflect contractual procurement and green power purchases ([Corporate Standard, p.27]).
    *   *What to do:* Report both location-based and market-based Scope 2. For market-based figures, use supplier-specific factors or energy attribute certificates (RECs/PPAs), applying the residual mix elsewhere.
*   **Reporting periods don't match across scopes**
    *   *What's wrong:* Scope 1 and Scope 2 cover only 3 months (2024-01 to 2024-03), while Scope 3 covers 12 months (2024-01 to 2024-12).
    *   *Why it matters:* Consistent tracking over time requires comparing emissions over the same defined annual period [Corporate Standard, p.37].
    *   *What to do:* Collect Scope 1 and Scope 2 data for the full calendar year.

### Medium Severity Findings

*   **Possible double counting of plant steam (Scope 1 coal boilers vs. Scope 2 steam)**
    *   *What's wrong:* The Manufacturing Plant reports coal boiler operations in Scope 1 and 1,887.0 tCO2e of process heating steam in Scope 2. If the steam is generated on-site, it is already captured in Scope 1.
    *   *Why it matters:* Scope 2 only covers purchased energy brought into the organizational boundary [Corporate Standard, p.27]. Counting the same generation in both scopes creates double counting.
    *   *What to do:* Verify whether steam is purchased via third-party contract or generated on-site. Remove it from Scope 2 if generated by owned boilers.
*   **Steam uses a generic factor and grid-region labels**
    *   *What's wrong:* Steam accounts for 2,116.0 tCO2e (84% of Scope 2), yet every site uses a flat factor of 0.074 t/MMBtu and is tagged with electricity grid regions like CAISO or PJM.
    *   *Why it matters:* Steam is not an electricity grid product, making regional grid tags inapplicable.
    *   *What to do:* Obtain supplier-specific steam factors reflecting actual fuel mix and boiler efficiency.
*   **Renewable share recorded but not reflected in emissions**
    *   *What's wrong:* Rows with notes indicating increased renewables continue to use standard grid factors.
    *   *What to do:* Determine whether renewables represent on-site generation (which reduces grid consumption) or contractual procurement, and adjust calculations accordingly.
